The Gulf · Lead story

THREE TANKERS ABLAZE AS THE GULF WAR AT SEA WIDENS

Iran says three ships hit mines east of Hormuz — and claims drone strikes on a US base in Kuwait.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said three oil tankers caught fire after striking mines east of the Strait of Hormuz, accusing the ships of sailing a restricted lane with their trackers off. The claim, carried by Iranian state media, could not be independently verified.

Tehran also claimed drone strikes on US Patriot missile systems at Ali Al Salem air base in Kuwait, calling it payback for the American strike on Qeshm Island. Arab states condemned the attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain — and the strike on a UAE tanker off Oman, the Navig8 Messi, left one crew member missing and eleven hurt. Eighteen ships have now been hit around the strait this month. Oil prices eased as traders weighed the escalation.

Shenzhen · Diplomacy

THREE AT ONE TABLE

Trump, Xi and Putin meet today in Shenzhen — the first trilateral summit of the three leaders, on the sidelines of APEC. Nuclear arms and the Iran war top the agenda; Ukraine is pointedly not on it. Officials on all sides say no deal is expected: the last US–Russian arms treaty lapsed in February, and China calls three-way disarmament talks unrealistic for now.

Crimea · War in Ukraine

THE BRIDGE, HIT AGAIN

Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge for the third time in three days, in the early hours of Thursday, the Moscow-backed governor of Crimea confirmed. Traffic was halted while damage was assessed, days after a strike collapsed a road span into the strait. It lands hours before Putin sits down with Trump and Xi in Shenzhen.

The Hague · Justice

THE VERDICT TODAY

The world court rules today on whether Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, in the case brought by The Gambia seven years ago. The judges read their judgment in The Hague — the first time the court could find a state, rather than an individual, guilty of genocide. Myanmar denies the charge; the ruling is final and binding.

Culture · Games

VICE CITY OPENS

Grand Theft Auto VI is out. After thirteen years, Rockstar’s sequel unlocks at midnight in each market, starting in New Zealand and rolling around the world on PlayStation 5 and Xbox. Pre-orders passed 16 million copies, worth over $1.5 billion, and early reviews put it at 99 on Metacritic — among the highest-scored games ever.
